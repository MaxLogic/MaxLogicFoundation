# Nia Marrowglass

## Status

Nia is being built like a PC-level important NPC.

Confirmed choices so far.

## Role

Important NPC, not a Wicked One PC.

Human warlock, Veyr Marrowglass's natural daughter, secret betrayer of Marrowglass Tower, protected key to the Black Cradle.

## Calling

Warlock.

## Core ability

**Sorcery**.

## Sorcery path

**Illusion**.

Nia's magic should focus on hiding, misdirection, false images, altered perception, softened sounds, concealed routes, false calm, and survival through making the world look slightly wrong.

She is not primarily a blaster.

## Starting Warlock ability

**Counterspell**.

On a success when resisting magical effects, Nia may choose one of the Counterspell benefits:

- absorb the spell and clear 1 stress,
- establish a quirk about the caster's power,
- reflect the spell at the caster.

## Normal actions

- **Invoke 3** — primary magic action, Illusion spells, wards, and defensive sorcery.
- **Trick 2** — lies, misdirection, covering guilt, social survival.
- **Scan 2** — reading notes, ruins, people, ritual clues, and Marrowglass procedures.
- **Skulk 1** — hiding, sneaking, staying alive.
- **Banter 1** — human conversation, calming minions, and small diplomacy.

## Gear defense

**Defense against magic**.

Nia's warded cloak, ritual scars, counterspell training, and built-in Mind Shield let her avoid or deflect magical consequences once per cycle when the defense applies.

## Supply

- **Father's cipher key** — a small Marrowglass code-wheel, used to decode Veyr's notes, lab labels, old ward instructions, and partial ritual logs.
- **Black thread and chalk** — used to mark illusion anchors, silent lines, ward boundaries, and false seams in walls or floors.
- **Small mirror charm** — used for misdirection, checking glamours, catching reflected glyphs, and detecting when an illusion is looking back.

## Dark impulse

**Paranoid** — Nia assumes any safety is temporary and every kindness has a cost.

This usually manifests quietly: checking exits, hiding useful facts until she knows who needs them, sleeping lightly, flinching from locked rooms, and preparing excuses before anyone asks questions.

## Revelry

**Occult**.

Nia lives it up by disappearing into forbidden notes, ward diagrams, sealed correspondence, half-burned spellbooks, and small private rituals. Her revelry is not loud. It is the kind of quiet that leaves candlewax, blood-specks, and three new lies by morning.

## Appearance

Nia is pretty in a quiet, almost accidental way. Not polished, not seductive, not courtly. More like the girl from the next house who learned too early to keep her voice down.

She has dark brown hair, usually tied back in a careless braid that never quite holds. Her skin is pale, and her features are soft rather than striking.

Her eyes are Veyr's sharp grey-green, which makes looking at her uncomfortable for anyone who knew him well. She has the same eyes, but none of his certainty.

She is slender and fragile-looking, with narrow wrists and a tendency to fold her arms close to herself when frightened. Her hands are ink-stained, scarred by small ritual cuts, and often hidden under fingerless gloves.

Her clothes are practical: a black traveling dress or fitted tunic, worn boots, a patched Marrowglass cloak, belt pouches, and old ward-thread sewn into the cuffs. Nothing decorative except a small mirror charm at her throat.

The broken part is not obvious at first. It shows in small ways:

- she flinches before doors close,
- she counts exits without noticing,
- she stops breathing when someone says **Father**,
- she apologizes too quickly,
- she smiles like she is trying not to be a burden,
- she sometimes looks at the Wicked Ones with a tenderness that immediately frightens her.

When she casts illusion magic, shadows gather around her fingers like threads pulled through water. Her magic is not flashy. It is a lie whispered so carefully that the world decides to believe it.

## Mind Shield

### Source

Veyr tampered with Nia when she was young.

He did not do it to protect her privacy. He did it because her mind had to survive contact with the Black Cradle.

The Mind Shield is a layered Marrowglass construct built into her:

- ritual scars,
- buried command words,
- bloodline locks,
- false memory partitions,
- pain-conditioned reflex wards,
- a psychic deadspace around her deepest thoughts.

It is part protection, part lock, part cage.

### Rule

**Mind Shield:** Whenever someone attempts to read, probe, extract, scan, or invade Nia's mind, she gets a **free resistance roll**.

- Roll her best relevant resistance action, usually **Invoke 3**.
- She gets **one free reroll** and keeps the better result.
- This costs her **no stress**.
- A critical does **not** clear stress; this prevents stress farming.
- The shield does not block mundane observation: posture, fear, lies, breathing, hesitation, and emotional tells still exist.

### Results

- **Critical:** She blocks the intrusion and learns something useful about the intruder: emotional state, magical flavor, psychic signature, or direction of attack.
- **Success:** She blocks the intrusion cleanly.
- **Mixed:** She blocks the real secret, but something leaks: fear, a symbolic image, pain, one misleading memory, or Black Cradle resonance.
- **Failure:** The intruder gets a fractured glimpse, not full access. The shield still prevents a clean read, but it creates a complication.

Possible fractured glimpses:

- a locked black door,
- Veyr's hand on her forehead,
- the Black Cradle opening like ribs,
- Nia whispering **I want to live**,
- a false memory Veyr planted.

## Cradle Key

Nia is not chosen in a heroic sense.

She is compatible material.

Veyr tuned her blood, body, and mind to the Black Cradle. That makes her useful and dangerous.

### Rule

**Black Cradle Key:** Nia can interact with the Black Cradle in ways others cannot.

She can:

- read some Cradle glyphs instinctively,
- feel its direction or activity,
- open minor seals with blood,
- stabilize Cradle-related rituals,
- suppress a Cradle surge briefly,
- substitute her blood for one ritual requirement in Cradle or Marrowglass-Cradle workings.

### Cost clock

Create a 6-clock:

**The Cradle Recognizes Nia**

Tick it when:

- she uses blood to activate a Cradle lock,
- she stabilizes the Cradle under pressure,
- she casts through it,
- someone performs a ritual using her blood,
- she sleeps near it during occult revelry,
- the Cradle is damaged while she is nearby.

Suggested ticks:

- controlled use: 1 tick,
- risky use: 2 ticks,
- desperate use or failed roll: 3 ticks.

When full, the Cradle makes a claim:

- it marks her body,
- opens when she does not want it to,
- calls to its original ritual,
- reveals her connection to someone,
- gives Caeloran or another enemy a way to track her,
- demands she enter the folded black altar.

This makes her useful without making the Cradle her superpower. She is a keyhole shaped like a person.

## Patron / blackmailer

Use **Caeloran Whitewake** as the visible blackmailer.

He is the white wizard rival of Veyr. Publicly, he is a high wizard, protector, scholar, and moral counterweight to Marrowglass cruelty.

Privately, he is controlled, cold, and willing to use Nia as gently as Veyr used her brutally.

### What happened

Caeloran found out that Veyr intended to use Nia as the living vessel for the final Black Cradle ritual.

He contacted her secretly and offered her a way out.

He told her:

**Open the wards. Leave the monsters away from the tower. I will stop him.**

Nia believed enough of it.

Then the Covenant attacked, Veyr died, the tower fell, and the monster-children survived.

Now Caeloran has proof that Nia helped him.

### His leverage

Caeloran has:

- written proof of Nia's betrayal,
- the counterphrase to one layer of her Mind Shield,
- research showing the Cradle ritual can still happen,
- evidence that the Wicked Ones were meant to be harvested for parts,
- perhaps one missing page from Veyr's ritual log.

### What he wants

Caeloran does not simply want Nia dead.

He wants:

- Veyr's logs,
- the Black Cradle,
- access to the underworld dungeon,
- proof that Veyr's creations can be controlled or destroyed,
- and eventually Nia back under **protective custody**.

### Deeper layer

The Black Cradle also whispers to Nia later.

Campaign structure:

- **Caeloran** is the active blackmailer.
- **The Black Cradle** is the deeper horror.
- **Nia** is trapped between survival, guilt, and attachment to the monsters she betrayed.

## Relationships with the Wicked Ones

### Shogoth

Nia is afraid of Shogoth, but less than she should be.

His inability to read her mind makes him strangely safer than others. With everyone else, she has to guard her face, voice, posture, and words. With Shogoth, she knows the main door is locked.

That does not mean she trusts him.

It means she can breathe near him.

She calls him **Archivist**, not thing, monster, or Father's creature.

She knows he protects her because of Veyr's order. She fears the day he decides the order ended.

### Beoruna

Beoruna is the one Nia feels safest near.

He is simple enough that her usual defenses do not work on him. She cannot talk him into abstraction. If he says **stand behind me**, she stands behind him.

She once said **please**, and he kept her secret.

That hurts her more than suspicion would.

He is kindness she did not earn.

### Tinch

Tinch makes her laugh.

That is inconvenient.

She reads labels for him, translates bad diagrams, stops him from mixing things that should not meet, and quietly corrects his math when he is three explosions deep into inspiration.

He thinks she is useful and nice.

She knows he has already noticed too much about the Cradle.

She is protective of him in a practical older-sister way, though she would deny the word **sister** if asked.

### Misha

Misha is dangerous because she does not need mind-reading.

She reads shame.

Nia likes her, fears her, and avoids being alone with her when tired. Misha sees the pauses before lies, the over-careful thanks, the way Nia touches her glove when the Cradle is mentioned.

Misha may become the first person Nia almost confesses to.

Almost.

### Xeno Zul

Xeno distrusts Nia openly.

Nia almost appreciates it.

At least with Xeno there is no pretending. He says she is a strategic liability. He says she is hiding something. He says her survival creates unacceptable variables.

He is correct often enough to be irritating.

His Vision through the Shogoth tether sometimes glitches near her Mind Shield, producing blind spots, false angles, or Cradle-shaped distortions. Xeno hates this. Nia hates that he notices.

## Current design notes

Counterspell fits Nia because she survived Veyr, Marrowglass Tower, the Black Cradle, and hostile magic by being prepared rather than by being physically strong.

It also supports the idea that her Mind Shield is not just passive protection, but part of a broader defensive magical discipline.

Nia's action spread makes her useful but not physically dominant. She is strongest when dealing with illusion, wards, secrets, misdirection, Marrowglass procedures, and dangerous magical systems.

Her paranoia should make her cautious and secretive, not openly villainous. She should remain useful, wounded, competent, and slightly tragic.

Nia should matter emotionally and tactically, but should not become the protagonist. Her role is a dangerous protected secret, not party leader.
